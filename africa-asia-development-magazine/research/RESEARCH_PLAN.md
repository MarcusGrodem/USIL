# Research Plan

## Goal

Build a source base strong enough to support the magazine's economic comparison, colonial analysis, cultural discussion, country cases and three original theories.

## Research principles

1. Start with comparable data before interpretation.
2. Prefer primary datasets and peer-reviewed research.
3. Record exact pages, tables and indicators.
4. Use the same definitions and years across comparisons where possible.
5. Separate regional patterns from country cases.
6. Search for evidence that challenges the preferred explanation.
7. Record uncertainty rather than filling gaps with assumptions.
8. Treat the class survey as a learning activity, not evidence about nations.

## Evidence hierarchy

### Tier 1: Primary and authoritative data

- World Bank World Development Indicators
- AfDB datasets and formal reports
- UNCTAD trade data
- UNESCO education data
- ADB data
- Maddison Project Database
- national statistical agencies where needed

### Tier 2: Peer-reviewed and high-quality academic research

- published journal articles;
- academic books;
- final published versions of working papers;
- recognised historical and country studies.

### Tier 3: Institutional interpretation

- World Bank, AfDB, UNCTAD and similar analytical reports;
- reputable policy institutes;
- official country histories.

### Tier 4: Background and design inspiration

- explanatory websites;
- magazine articles;
- educational videos;
- visual references.

Tier 4 can help explain or design. It should not carry the main causal claims.

## Priority indicators

- GDP per capita or another clearly defined income measure
- labour productivity
- agriculture employment share
- manufacturing employment or value-added share
- services employment or value-added share
- urban population share
- literacy or school attainment
- electricity access or supply reliability
- merchandise export composition
- manufactured export share
- commodity concentration
- domestic and regional trade
- logistics or transport costs where available
- firm size, ownership or formality where comparable
- institutional and trust measures only when definitions are credible

## Comparison rules

- State whether values are nominal, real, PPP or constant-price.
- Do not mix country values with regional averages in the same visual without clear labelling.
- Use identical axes for paired charts.
- Explain missing data.
- Do not infer causality from a trend line alone.
- Record major breaks in national boundaries or statistical methods.

## Country-case template

Use the same headings for every case:

1. Starting position
2. Colonial or historical inheritance
3. Political and institutional context
4. Education and skills
5. Infrastructure and market connection
6. Industrial and trade strategy
7. Firm learning and upgrading
8. Cultural mechanisms relevant to the case
9. External support or constraints
10. Outcomes
11. What the case supports
12. What the case challenges
13. What cannot be concluded

## Case selection criteria

The second African case and additional Asian cases should be selected using:

- analytical relevance to at least one group theory;
- availability of reliable data;
- difference from existing cases;
- ability to challenge a simple explanation;
- manageable scope;
- visual storytelling potential.

## Colonialism research tasks

- Build a map of colonial control with a clear date.
- Record independence dates.
- Identify transport networks and original purposes.
- Compare British and French administrative tendencies using academic sources.
- Add at least one exception to each colonial model.
- Identify one concrete divided-border example.
- Explain how inherited state capacity differed across cases.
- Compare African and Asian colonial experiences without claiming they were identical.

## Culture research tasks

- Locate Hofstede's original or authoritative dimension definitions.
- Find a credible critique of national cultural scores.
- Identify which dimensions have a plausible mechanism in this project.
- Avoid using national scores where the country or period does not match the data.
- Distinguish trust from Hofstede's dimensions.
- Treat culture as changeable and affected by institutions and development.

## Original class survey

Possible questions:

- Would you hire a family member over a slightly more qualified stranger?
- Would you openly disagree with a manager who was wrong?
- Would you trust a signed contract with someone you had never met?
- Would you invest money today for a benefit in ten years?
- Should the same rule apply equally to everyone?

Required limitations:

- anonymous participation;
- record sample size;
- describe who was surveyed;
- do not generalise beyond the class;
- do not assign national culture labels to individuals;
- use the result to start discussion, not prove causality.
