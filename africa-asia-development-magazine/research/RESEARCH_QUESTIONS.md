# Research Questions

## Primary question

> Why did many African and Asian economies follow different development paths, and how much can culture explain?

## Recurring question

> Was slower growth caused by culture, or was culture itself partly shaped by history and institutions?

This question should appear throughout the magazine and return in the conclusion.

## Economic questions

- What exactly diverged: income, productivity, industrial structure, exports, capabilities or all of these?
- Which African and Asian countries had reasonably comparable starting conditions?
- Did urbanisation lead to manufacturing expansion, low-productivity services or both?
- How did export composition change after independence?
- How much labour moved out of agriculture, and where did it go?
- What role did firm learning and technology upgrading play?
- Which indicators can be compared consistently across countries and time?

## Colonialism questions

- What did newly independent African states inherit around 1960?
- How did colonial borders affect identity, political competition and trust?
- Were transport networks built to connect domestic markets or to move resources to ports?
- How did indirect rule differ from centralised colonial administration?
- How did pre-colonial institutions shape the way colonial rule worked?
- Did British and French education systems leave different outcomes?
- Why did colonial legacies produce different outcomes across Africa and Asia?
- Which effects remained after independence, and which were changed by later policy?

## Industrialisation questions

- Why did several East Asian economies build export manufacturing faster?
- How important were education, infrastructure, state coordination and external markets?
- How did firms move from simple production into more complex products?
- What role did Cold War support or other geopolitics play?
- Why did similar policies succeed in some countries and fail in others?
- Which parts of East Asian experience can be adapted elsewhere?
- Which parts depend on historical or political conditions that cannot be copied?

## Cultural questions

- How does trust affect contracts, delegation, banking and firm size?
- When do family networks support entrepreneurship, and when can obligations restrict scaling?
- How might power distance affect management, public accountability and willingness to challenge leaders?
- How might long-term orientation relate to saving, education and industrial investment?
- Could uncertainty avoidance influence regulation, entrepreneurship or reactions to reform?
- Are masculinity/femininity and indulgence/restraint relevant enough to support an economic claim?
- Do cultural values shape institutions, or do institutions shape cultural values?
- Can economic development itself change culture?
- How much variation exists inside countries that Hofstede scores hide?

## Questions for the three original theories

### Connected Development Theory

- What if the important difference was not the amount of infrastructure, but whom it connected?
- Do stronger internal and regional networks predict more diversification?
- Can a country industrialise rapidly while remaining internally disconnected?
- How should digital, energy and institutional connection be treated alongside roads and railways?

### Radius of Trust Theory

- How far beyond family and close networks can economic trust travel?
- Does broader institutional trust support larger formal firms and greater delegation?
- Can strong contracts or enforcement substitute for low interpersonal trust?
- Can low-trust settings scale firms through family conglomerates, state ownership or foreign capital?

### Continuity + Adaptation Theory

- Can a country learn industrially if its direction changes every few years?
- Which policies need continuity, and which should change quickly?
- How can a state preserve long-term direction without protecting failure?
- Do countries with frequent policy reversal consistently show weaker capability building?

## Case-study questions

### South Korea and Ghana

- How similar were their starting positions, and where were they already different?
- What were their export structures?
- How did education and state capacity differ?
- What external support or geopolitical conditions differed?
- How stable were their industrial strategies?
- What can this comparison explain, and what can it not prove?

### Botswana

- Which simple theory is Botswana meant to challenge?
- Which institutions, resources or historical conditions made its path different?
- Does Botswana support or challenge any of the three group theories?
- Which outcomes count as success, and which limitations remain?

### Second African case

- Which candidate offers the strongest test of the theories?
- Does the case show manufacturing, services, resource-led growth or another path?
- Does it add a genuinely different mechanism?

### Variation within Asia

- Which Asian economies did not follow the fast-growth path?
- Which mechanisms were absent, interrupted or replaced?
- Does the variation weaken a broad culture-based explanation?

## Reader-facing questions

These can be used as headlines, pull quotes, polls or page openers.

- If natural resources create wealth, why do resource-rich countries have such different outcomes?
- Would you build a railway to the port or roads between cities?
- If you became president in 1960, where would you spend the first 100 development points?
- Who would you trust to manage your company's money?
- Would you hire a qualified stranger or a slightly less-qualified relative?
- Would you challenge a manager who was clearly wrong?
- Is giving a job to a relative always corruption?
- Can a policy be stable for too long?
- Which country would force us to abandon our theory?
- If culture matters, can culture change?
