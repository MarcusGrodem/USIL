# AI Context

## What this project is

This repository supports a student team creating an approximately 25-page magazine and presentation about why many African economies did not follow the same development path as several Asian economies. Final pagination may vary slightly for references, credits, and front or back matter. The assignment specifically requires cultural analysis, multiple cultural dimensions, research quality, examples, creativity and innovation.

The current project is an investigation, not a simple continent ranking. It combines economic development, colonial history, institutions, industrialisation, culture and original group theories.

## What has already been decided

- The main title direction is **Africa & Asia: Why Did Their Economic Paths Diverge?**
- The main question asks why selected African and Asian economies followed different paths and how much culture can explain.
- The magazine must not claim that African culture caused slow growth.
- Africa and Asia must not be treated as homogeneous regions.
- Colonialism is a main section, not a footnote.
- South Korea and Ghana are the main paired cases.
- Botswana is a counterexample.
- Mauritius is locked as the second African case.
- All six Hofstede dimensions must appear.
- Hofstede must be used critically.
- The group will test three original working theories:
  - Connected Development Theory
  - Radius of Trust Theory
  - Continuity + Adaptation Theory
- The theories should be introduced through questions and challenged with evidence.
- The final design should use the dedicated palette in `design/africa_asia_colour_palette.md`.
- The 45-page PDF is a legacy structural draft and idea bank, not the current page plan, final evidence, or final visual design.
- Build notes and infographic/interaction tips are useful working elements.
- Canva and Genially are the preferred production tools.

## What should not be changed without a good reason

- Do not return to a single culture-first explanation.
- Do not describe Africa or Asia as one culture.
- Do not expand the current three theories back into a long list unless new evidence requires it.
- Do not present the group's theories as established academic frameworks.
- Do not remove counterexamples.
- Do not use Hofstede scores as proof of causality.
- Do not use placeholder numbers or diagrams from the PDF as facts.
- Do not replace the final colour palette with the rough PDF palette without documenting the decision.
- Do not choose a second African case only because it appears successful; it must test the analysis.

## Important constraints

- Approximately 25 pages, with slight variation allowed for references, credits, and front or back matter.
- Magazine style.
- Sources required.
- Creative and innovative presentation.
- Use of multiple cultural dimensions.
- Weekly research reviews.
- Any team member may be chosen by the teacher to present.
- The final report should be analytical, not merely descriptive.
- No invented facts, sources, data or quotations.
- Exact presentation length is not known. APA 7 is the project's required citation style.

## Tone and style

- Clear, direct and student-readable.
- Curious and investigative.
- Short magazine copy rather than academic padding.
- Questions before conclusions.
- Specific cases and mechanisms rather than broad moral claims.
- Critical without becoming cynical.
- Avoid stereotypes, deficit framing and "Asia good / Africa bad" language.
- Use visual hierarchy, captions, pull quotes and short explanations.

## Known uncertainties

- Detailed evidence for Mauritius, the locked second African case.
- Detailed evidence for Malaysia and the Philippines.
- Exact indicator definitions and treatment of missing anchor-year data.
- Which interactive elements the team can complete.
- Final publication platform.
- Presentation length.
- Any teacher-requested exception to the project's APA 7 standard.
- Team roles and deadlines.

## Working rules for future AI contributions

- Label proposed facts as unverified until a source is recorded.
- Distinguish source evidence, inference and group hypothesis.
- Prefer primary data providers and peer-reviewed research.
- Use consistent indicators and years in comparisons.
- Add sources to `SOURCES.md` as soon as they enter the project.
- Update `DECISIONS.md` when direction changes.
- Update `ROADMAP.md` when work is completed or reprioritised.
- Preserve the original question-led structure.
- When suggesting a graphic, state what claim it helps the reader understand.
