# Decision Log

This file records decisions that were actually made during the project conversation. Later decisions override earlier ones.

## Decision: Use a visual magazine rather than a conventional report

**Decision:**  
Build an editorial magazine of approximately 25 pages using short analysis, images, maps, charts, cases, interactive exercises and QR-linked material. Final pagination may vary slightly for references, credits, and front or back matter.

**Reason:**  
The assignment rewards creativity and innovation, and the teacher clarified that the pages do not need to be dense written pages.

**Alternatives considered:**  
The earlier minimum-40-page requirement and 45-page production plan; a traditional text-heavy report.

**Status:**  
Current as of 2026-10-07; supersedes the earlier 40/45-page criteria.

## Decision: Reframe the continental comparison

**Decision:**  
Ask why many African economies followed different development paths from several East and Southeast Asian economies, rather than treating Africa and Asia as single units.

**Reason:**  
Both continents contain major differences. A broad "Africa versus Asia" explanation would be academically weak and vulnerable to stereotypes.

**Alternatives considered:**  
A direct continent-wide comparison with one explanation.

**Status:**  
Current.

## Decision: Culture will not be treated as the sole cause

**Decision:**  
Analyse culture alongside colonial history, institutions, geography, industrial policy, education, infrastructure and trade.

**Reason:**  
The evidence discussed does not support a single culture-first explanation. Culture and institutions may shape one another.

**Alternatives considered:**  
An argument that collectivism, power distance or another cultural trait directly caused slower growth.

**Status:**  
Current. The simple culture-first alternative is rejected.

## Decision: Colonialism is a main analytical section

**Decision:**  
Use colonialism as a central bridge between economic structures and cultural or institutional behaviour.

**Reason:**  
Colonial rule affected borders, infrastructure, state systems, education, languages, export structures and authority relationships inherited at independence.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Treating colonialism as a short historical background section.

**Status:**  
Current.

## Decision: British and French rule are tendencies, not fixed templates

**Decision:**  
Compare indirect-rule and centralised-administration tendencies while showing that implementation differed by territory and interacted with pre-colonial institutions.

**Reason:**  
Simple claims such as "British colonies did better" are not supported by the discussion and ignore variation.

**Alternatives considered:**  
A binary ranking of British and French colonial systems.

**Status:**  
Current.

## Decision: Use country cases with different analytical jobs

**Decision:**  
Use South Korea and Ghana as the main comparison, Botswana as a counterexample, one additional African case, and several contrasting Asian trajectories.

**Reason:**  
Country cases make the mechanisms visible and prevent a continental generalisation.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Only regional averages or a long list of shallow country examples.

**Status:**  
Superseded in part by `SCOPE_LOCK.md`: Mauritius is the second African case; Malaysia and the Philippines join South Korea as the Asian cases.

## Decision: Include all six Hofstede dimensions

**Decision:**  
Include power distance, individualism/collectivism, uncertainty avoidance, masculinity/femininity, long-/short-term orientation and indulgence/restraint.

**Reason:**  
The teacher specifically presented the six-dimension framework.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Using only trust, individualism, power distance, long-term orientation and universalism/particularism.

**Status:**  
Current. Trust remains a major lens even though it is not one of Hofstede's six dimensions. Universalism/particularism is now secondary rather than a main page.

## Decision: Use Hofstede critically

**Decision:**  
Present Hofstede as a comparison lens, not a direct explanation of growth or a description of every individual.

**Reason:**  
National averages can hide variation and cannot establish causation by themselves.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Using Hofstede scores as a simple ranking of development-friendly and development-unfriendly cultures.

**Status:**  
Current. The ranking approach is rejected.

## Decision: Develop original group theories

**Decision:**  
The magazine will develop its own working hypotheses and test them with cases, data and counterexamples.

**Reason:**  
The teacher asked the group to create its own theories.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Only applying Hofstede and established development theories.

**Status:**  
Current.

## Decision: Reduce five theories to three

**Decision:**  
Use Connected Development Theory, Radius of Trust Theory and Continuity + Adaptation Theory.

**Reason:**  
The user felt that five theories made the project too theory-heavy and wanted two or three theories supported by questions and statements.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Five theories, including separate Institutional Reach and Pressure-to-Upgrade theories.

**Status:**  
Current. The five-theory version was changed.

## Decision: Introduce theories as questions

**Decision:**  
Make readers encounter each theory first through an open question, then show evidence and possible challenges.

**Reason:**  
The goal is to make the reader consider explanations rather than being told what to believe.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Presenting the theories as settled conclusions at the beginning.

**Status:**  
Current.

## Decision: Every theory must be testable

**Decision:**  
Each theory needs a mechanism, prediction, evidence, counterexample, limitation and a condition that would require revision.

**Reason:**  
An original theory should be more than an opinion.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Broad statements that cannot be challenged.

**Status:**  
Current.

## Decision: Use interactive experiments

**Decision:**  
Include decision games, class polls, a border exercise, a president-in-1960 budget game, trust choices and myth-or-fact cards.

**Reason:**  
These devices make theory and evidence memorable and support the class presentation.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Standard quizzes placed after the theory.

**Status:**  
Current. The team still needs to decide how many can be built well.

## Decision: Canva and Genially are the preferred production tools

**Decision:**  
Use Canva for the main magazine and Genially for interactive scenarios.

**Reason:**  
The combination offers a strong balance of visual quality, accessibility and no-code interaction.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Flipsnack for the whole publication, Adobe Express for video, and GDevelop for a full game.

**Status:**  
Current preference. Flipsnack and Adobe Express remain optional; GDevelop is only justified if the team has time and skill.

## Decision: Use build notes as production guidance

**Decision:**  
Keep a build note on working pages and add a separate infographic or interaction tip.

**Reason:**  
The user found the build notes useful and asked for concrete diagram and visual suggestions.

**Alternatives considered:**  
A clean storyboard without production guidance.

**Status:**  
Current in the rough PDF.

## Decision: Adopt the dedicated colour palette

**Decision:**  
Use the terracotta, ochre and green Africa palette; red, blue and jade Asia palette; and cream, charcoal and grey shared base.

**Reason:**  
A dedicated palette guide was created after the first PDF and is the newer design decision.

**Alternatives considered:**  
The navy, bright orange, teal and gold palette used in the generated rough PDF.

**Status:**  
Current. The PDF palette is superseded for final design, although the PDF remains valid as a structural draft.

## Decision: Treat the rough PDF as a storyboard, not evidence

**Decision:**  
Use the legacy 45-page PDF only as an idea bank for hierarchy, interactions and visual density. Follow the current approximately 25-page plan for page order and pagination. Replace all placeholder data, images, maps and QR codes.

**Reason:**  
The PDF was created to estimate structure and illustration needs, not to provide final research.

**Alternatives considered:**  
Using the draft charts and statements directly in the final submission.

**Status:**  
Current. Direct use of unsourced placeholders is rejected.
