# Presentation Plan

The teacher will choose one member to represent the team. Every member therefore needs to understand the complete argument, not only the pages they researched.

The presentation length was not provided in the conversation. Adjust the structure below when the teacher confirms the time.

## Presentation principle

Begin with a decision, not a definition.

The strongest opening is an interaction that makes the audience commit to an answer before the team explains the research.

Possible opening:

> You are president of a newly independent country in 1960. You have 100 development points. Where do you spend them?

Show the choices, collect a quick vote, then reveal the inherited constraints.

## Core presentation sequence

### 1. The puzzle

- Many countries in Africa and Asia were poor and largely agricultural around the middle of the twentieth century.
- Several Asian economies industrialised much faster.
- Why did the paths diverge?

### 2. The guardrail

- Africa and Asia are not single cultures.
- The project compares mechanisms and selected countries.
- Culture is one part of the explanation.

### 3. The inherited starting line

- colonial borders;
- extractive infrastructure;
- commodity dependence;
- thin industrial bases;
- different state and education systems.

### 4. The East Asian industrial turn

- education and skills;
- infrastructure;
- export manufacturing;
- firm learning;
- long-term upgrading;
- important variation between countries.

### 5. The cultural layer

- Hofstede as a lens, not proof;
- trust, family networks, authority and time horizons;
- two-way relationship between culture and institutions.

### 6. The group's three theories

- Connected Development
- Radius of Trust
- Continuity + Adaptation

For each theory, explain:

1. the question;
2. the mechanism;
3. one piece of supporting evidence;
4. one challenge or limitation.

### 7. The counterexample

Use Botswana or the second African case to show how a simple first explanation fails and must be revised.

### 8. Conclusion

- no single cause explains the divergence;
- colonial history changed the starting conditions;
- industrial capability requires more than growth in GDP;
- cultural behaviour works through institutions and can change;
- policies can be adapted, but historical conditions cannot simply be copied.

End with:

> If culture matters, can culture change?

## Recommended live interaction

Choose one main interaction for the presentation. Do not overload the audience.

Best current options:

1. President-in-1960 budget allocation
2. Who would you trust with the money?
3. Rank the strongest explanation before and after the presentation

The ranking activity has the clearest link to learning because the class can see whether its view changed.

## Presenter preparation

Every team member should be able to answer:

- What is the main question?
- Why is "Africa versus Asia" too broad?
- What is structural transformation?
- Why is colonialism not a complete explanation?
- Why is culture not a complete explanation?
- What are Hofstede's six dimensions?
- What are the three group theories?
- What evidence supports and challenges each theory?
- Why compare South Korea and Ghana?
- Why use Botswana as a counterexample?
- What is the final conclusion?

## Shared cue sheet

Create a one-page cue sheet containing:

- one sentence per section;
- the three theory questions;
- three essential numbers;
- one warning about causality;
- one counterexample;
- the final five takeaways;
- full names of the most important sources.

## Weekly research-review format

Each week, present one step in solving the puzzle.

Suggested structure:

1. **Question:** What did we investigate this week?
2. **Finding:** What is the strongest new evidence?
3. **Visual:** Show one chart, map, page mock-up or interaction.
4. **Theory impact:** Which theory became stronger, weaker or more complicated?
5. **Next test:** What do we need to find next?

Example:

> This week we tested whether infrastructure quantity mattered more than network design. A colonial railway map suggests that many routes connected mines to ports rather than domestic markets. That supports Connected Development Theory, but we still need a case where a corridor created wider industrial spillovers.

## Likely questions from the teacher

- Are you blaming culture for poverty?
- How do you know culture caused the outcome?
- Why did some Asian colonies grow quickly?
- Why compare South Korea and Ghana?
- Is Hofstede still reliable?
- What would prove your own theories wrong?
- Which African countries do not fit your explanation?
- Are you describing all African countries the same way?
- Which lessons from Asia are actually transferable?

## Final presentation check

- Any team member can present.
- The argument is understandable without reading the magazine.
- Claims and numbers have sources.
- The interaction works quickly.
- Slides or magazine pages remain readable from the back of the room.
- The presenter explains uncertainty honestly.
- The conclusion answers the main question without pretending there is one cause.
