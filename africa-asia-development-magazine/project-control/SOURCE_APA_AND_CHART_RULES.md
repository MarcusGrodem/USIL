# APA 7 and Comparable-Graph Rules

## APA 7 source rules

A URL is not a finished citation. Every source record must contain:

- author or responsible organisation;
- date or `n.d.`;
- title in sentence case;
- journal, publisher, dataset, collection, or other container;
- volume, issue, pages, report number, version, or edition when applicable;
- DOI in `https://doi.org/...` format, otherwise a stable URL;
- retrieval date only for content designed to change;
- exact page, table, figure, chapter, indicator code, or reproducible query supporting the claim;
- linked claim IDs, planned magazine pages, verification date, and limitations.

In-text forms:

- Parenthetical: `(World Bank, 2024)`.
- Narrative: `World Bank (2024)`.
- Two authors: `(Author & Author, 2024)`.
- Three or more authors: `(Author et al., 2024)`.
- Direct quotations require a page or paragraph locator.

Common reference patterns:

- Journal: Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. *Journal Title, volume*(issue), xx–xx. https://doi.org/xxxxx
- Report: Organisation. (Year). *Title of report* (Report No.). URL
- Dataset: Organisation. (Year). *Dataset title* (Version) [Data set]. Publisher if different. URL
- Web page: Organisation. (Year, Month Day). *Page title*. Site if different. URL
- Image: Creator. (Year). *Title or description* [Photograph/Illustration/Map]. Collection. URL

Each chart needs a concise note plus a full bibliography entry. Example structure:

> *Note.* GDP per capita in constant 2015 US dollars. Data from *World Development Indicators* (indicator NY.GDP.PCAP.KD), by World Bank (year), URL. Values shown for 1960, 1990, and 2020.

Use `Data from` when the team creates a chart from data. Use APA's `From` or `Adapted from` language when reproducing or adapting a visual, and confirm reuse rights.

Source approval requires opening the original item, confirming that it supports the exact claim, recording the locator/query, completing the APA entry, and documenting limitations. Prefer a final peer-reviewed publication over a working-paper version when one exists.

## Comparable graph rules

The user-provided examples are inspiration for direct labels, matched panels, low clutter, and visible differences. They are not project evidence.

- Use identical definitions, units, price bases, years, axes, dimensions, and encodings for direct comparisons.
- When an anchor year is unavailable, print the actual proxy year and explain it. Never place a proxy as if it were the anchor observation.
- For two countries, use matched lines, slopegraphs, dot plots, or mirrored dashboards.
- For three to six countries, prefer small multiples with identical axes.
- Use a shared multi-line chart only when the interaction of the lines is the point; normally limit it to four series.
- Direct-label lines or panels. Avoid forcing the reader to search a legend.
- Start bars at zero. Clearly justify any truncated line-chart scale.
- Do not use 3D, dual axes, or decorative maps as quantitative evidence.
- Use a fixed country colour plus a label, marker, or pattern so colour is never the only identifier.
- Give graphs a message title, factual subtitle, unit, years, visible missing-data/proxy notes, and APA source note.
- Put “What this comparison cannot prove” beside major causal comparisons.

Each final graph package contains:

1. editable source;
2. publication export;
3. tidy underlying data;
4. specification with chart ID, question, indicator, unit, countries, years, scale, markers, annotations, and missing-data treatment;
5. APA figure note and complete references;
6. verifier and date.

Graph acceptance check:

- one clear question;
- approved values only;
- matched definitions, units, years, and scales or a visible exception;
- direct labels and readable values;
- colour-independent identification;
- visible gaps and proxies;
- saved data and APA note;
- legible on A4 and classroom projection;
- independent numeric check.
