# Design Guide

## Design goal

Create a contemporary editorial magazine that feels investigative, visual and thoughtful. It should not look like a 40-page classroom essay placed into a template.

The design should help readers compare evidence, notice uncertainty and follow the argument.

## Relationship to the rough PDF

The rough PDF uses an earlier navy, bright orange, teal and gold palette. Its structure, hierarchy and page ideas remain useful. Its colours should not be copied automatically into the final design.

## Typography

The final typefaces have not been selected.

Use no more than:

- one strong display face for section openers and major questions;
- one highly readable sans-serif for body copy, captions and data;
- an optional restrained accent style for pull quotes.

Priorities:

- easy reading at normal magazine size;
- clear numeric characters for charts;
- enough weights for hierarchy;
- support for all required characters;
- consistent use across Canva, charts and interactive content.

Do not choose typography only because it appears "African" or "Asian." Avoid decorative stereotypes.

## Layout system

- Use a consistent grid and margins.
- Use section colours as navigation, not decoration.
- Limit each page to one primary idea.
- Use full-page visuals for section transitions.
- Use matched layouts for direct comparisons.
- Keep long copy to a minority of pages.
- Use generous white space.
- Keep source notes visible but secondary.
- Use repeated motifs for the three theories.

## Comparison rules

- Use identical scales and time periods in paired charts.
- Label countries directly where possible.
- Do not encode Africa and Asia only through colour; also use labels, shapes or patterns.
- State whether a chart uses countries, selected cases or regional averages.
- Include a note when data are not fully comparable.
- Place "what this comparison cannot prove" near major case comparisons.

## Photography and illustration

- Use archival images with sources and dates.
- Give both regions equal visual dignity.
- Avoid images that reduce Africa to poverty, conflict or rural life.
- Avoid images that reduce Asia to factories, megacities or technology.
- Show people as workers, citizens, entrepreneurs, officials and students rather than anonymous symbols.
- Use illustration when a photograph would imply false precision.
- Credit every image.

## Maps

- State the date represented by historical maps.
- Label disputed or changing boundaries carefully.
- Use accurate source data.
- Do not use decorative continent silhouettes as evidence.
- Distinguish colonial control, ethnic territories, transport routes and modern borders.
- Include legends and sources.

## Charts

- Every chart needs a clear question or claim.
- Every chart needs units, years, source and data definition.
- Use direct labels when possible.
- Avoid three-dimensional effects.
- Avoid dual axes unless no clearer option exists.
- Use small multiples for country variation.
- Use annotation to explain major changes.
- Mark placeholder charts clearly during drafting.

## Pull quotes and questions

Questions are a core design device.

Use them to:

- open sections;
- introduce the three theories;
- invite a reader decision;
- challenge a simple conclusion;
- transition from evidence to interpretation.

Do not use a question when the magazine has no plan to investigate it.

## Build notes

The rough PDF contains yellow build notes with:

- a page action;
- an infographic or interaction tip.

Keep these in internal drafts. Remove them from the final student-facing magazine unless the team deliberately turns them into an editorial device.

## QR codes and interactions

- Use a descriptive label, not "scan me."
- Explain what the reader will receive.
- Test every link from a phone.
- Keep a static summary on the page for readers who cannot open the link.
- Do not use QR codes for basic information that belongs in the magazine.
- Use a consistent size and placement.

## Accessibility

- Maintain strong contrast.
- Do not rely on colour alone.
- Use readable type sizes.
- Add alternative text to digital images where the platform allows it.
- Caption videos.
- Keep interactions usable without sound.
- Provide a non-interactive version of essential findings.

## Source and credit style

Each research page should have either:

- a short source line connected to numbered endnotes; or
- a complete citation where space allows.

The final back matter should include:

- research bibliography;
- dataset citations;
- image credits;
- map sources;
- video or interaction credits;
- access dates where required.

## Visual quality checklist

- One main point per page
- Consistent margins and alignment
- Stable type hierarchy
- Palette used consistently
- Charts use verified data
- Maps are accurate and dated
- Sources are visible
- Images avoid stereotypes
- Comparison pages are truly comparable
- Interactions add analytical value
- Build notes removed from final export
