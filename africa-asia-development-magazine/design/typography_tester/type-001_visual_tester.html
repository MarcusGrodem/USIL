Three candidate typefaces for the Africa–Asia Development Magazine

Montserrat — strengths / watch-outs

Strengths. Confident geometric display; nine weights up to Black (900) for dramatic section openers; modern, friendly voice; wide language coverage; already common in modern infographics.

  • Needs a slightly lower body size than Inter at the same visual weight — set body at 9.5 pt, not 10 pt.
  • Tabular lining numerals supported but historically narrower; verify alignment in dense tables.
  • Can read as "startup template" if used at only one weight; use Black + Medium contrast to earn editorial feel.
Inter — strengths / watch-outs

Strengths. Humanist, screen-tuned, excellent at small sizes; variable axis for continuous weight control; a well-trodden choice in data journalism (FT, Pudding, Our World in Data). Numerals are purpose-built for charts.

  • Less distinctive display personality than Montserrat Black or Plex 700; may feel neutral on a cover.
  • Pair with a serif display face if the magazine wants more editorial character on openers.
  • Confirm the Display cuts, not only the base family, if used above 32 pt.
IBM Plex Sans — strengths / watch-outs

Strengths. Editorial-technical voice; corporate seriousness without stiffness; strong hierarchy at 600/700; condensed and mono siblings available for figure callouts and code-like captions; well tested in long-form editorial PDFs.

  • No weight above 700; display headlines rely on size and tracking rather than Black weight.
  • Numerals are high-contrast and clear but visibly "IBM"; may clash with Palette A warmth unless balanced by cream-heavy layouts.
  • Watch spacing on all-caps eyebrows at small sizes.
