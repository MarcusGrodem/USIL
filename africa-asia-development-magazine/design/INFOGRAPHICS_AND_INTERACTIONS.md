# Infographics and Interactions

This file converts the visual ideas from the conversation and rough PDF into a production list.

## Selection rule

Every visual or interaction should help answer a question. If it only decorates the page, it is optional.

## Front-of-book visuals

### Guiding-question motif

Repeat the central question as a pull quote at major section breaks:

> Was slower growth caused by culture, or was culture itself partly shaped by history and institutions?

### 1960 split screen

Use paired archival photographs and one shared timeline to establish the puzzle without implying that the regions were identical.

### Mirrored baseline dashboard

Show the same indicators with the same scales and years. Potential measures include income, agriculture, literacy, urbanisation and manufacturing.

### Structural-transformation flow

Use a Sankey-style diagram showing workers moving from agriculture into manufacturing or services. The graphic should distinguish higher- and lower-productivity destinations.

### Diversity map mosaic

Fill each continent shape with several country examples, not national flags alone. Use this to make the "not one story" warning visible.

## Three-theory visuals

### Theory-building cycle

```text
Observe -> Connect -> Predict -> Test -> Revise
```

Use a circle or stepped path. The final step should point back to observation.

### Three question cards

Create one card for each theory:

- Connected Development
- Radius of Trust
- Continuity + Adaptation

Each card contains:

1. the reader question;
2. one-sentence theory statement;
3. a vote or confidence slider.

### Before-and-after theory vote

Ask readers which idea seems strongest before the evidence and again near the conclusion. Show how the distribution changed.

### Theory test matrix

| Theory | Prediction | Supporting case | Challenge | Revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|

This is one of the most important analytical visuals in the project.

### Combined-theory Venn diagram

Three circles:

- connected markets;
- wider trust;
- continuity + adaptation.

Place industrial capability in the centre. Use only if the evidence justifies a combined conclusion.

## Colonialism visuals

### Colonial control map and independence timeline

Use an accurate map for a stated date. Pair it with a timeline from the Scramble for Africa to later independence.

### Authority-chain comparison

Mirror the layouts:

```text
British tendency: London -> governor -> chief/local authority -> community
French tendency: Paris -> governor -> central administration -> citizen
```

Add one exception beneath each chain.

### Four-empires card strip

Belgian, Portuguese, German and Italian cases. Each card contains:

- one archival image;
- one verified characteristic;
- one warning against overgeneralisation.

### Railway network comparison

Show:

- mine or plantation -> railway -> port;
- city <-> city <-> supplier <-> domestic market.

This visual directly supports Connected Development Theory.

### Border layers

Overlay cultural or political territories and modern state borders with transparent colours. Use one real example rather than a purely fictional map in the final report.

### Independence suitcase

Show what a new government inherits:

- export corridor;
- commodity dependence;
- limited manufacturing;
- divided or combined communities;
- colonial bureaucracy;
- skill and education gaps.

## Industrialisation visuals

### East Asian capability loop

```text
Education -> Firms -> Exports -> Learning -> Reinvestment -> Upgrading -> Education
```

Use country annotations to show that the sequence differed across cases.

### Product value-chain journey

Example:

```text
Raw cotton -> Yarn -> Fabric -> Garment -> Design -> Brand
```

Show where employment, knowledge and value are added.

### Matched country timelines

South Korea and Ghana should use the same four periods and visual structure. Each frame should contain:

- one policy or institutional change;
- one indicator;
- one image;
- one limit or complication.

### Comparison warning tags

Place two visible notes on the South Korea/Ghana comparison:

- Similar starting income does not mean identical starting conditions.
- A two-country comparison cannot isolate one cause.

## Culture visuals

### Hofstede six-dimension wheel

Include all six dimensions with a central caution:

> National averages are lenses, not descriptions of every individual.

### Trust-radius network

Start with family in the centre and expand toward:

- friends;
- professional managers;
- suppliers;
- banks;
- courts;
- government;
- strangers.

Use line weight to show trust or reliance, but only if based on evidence.

### Strength-versus-cost balance

For individualism/collectivism, power distance and long-term orientation, show a possible strength and a possible cost. Add the sentence:

> The effect depends on institutions, incentives and context.

### Manager-employee comic

Two panels:

1. employee openly challenges a wrong decision;
2. employee remains silent.

Ask what each response protects and what it risks.

### Time-horizon choice

Give the reader three options:

- consume today;
- invest for five years;
- invest for twenty years.

Then reveal why present needs and uncertainty may make long-term investment difficult.

## Interactive concepts

### 1. President in 1960

**Format:** Genially branching scenario  
**Input:** Allocate 100 points among schools, factories, roads, health, military and government capacity.  
**Output:** Growth, inequality, debt, trust and stability indicators.  
**Important:** There should be no obviously perfect answer.

### 2. Draw the border

**Format:** Drag a border line across groups and resources.  
**Output:** Consequences for trade, identity, administration and political competition.  
**Important:** Explain that the activity simplifies a complex historical process.

### 3. Who would you trust with the money?

**Choices:** Family member, close friend, professional manager, bank or government agency.  
**Reveal:** Personal versus institutional trust.  
**Important:** Do not assign national labels to individual answers.

### 4. Class culture survey

Use five anonymous questions on hiring, authority, contracts, time horizons and equal rules. Show sample size and limits.

### 5. Myth or fact

Possible cards:

- Culture explains everything.
- Colonialism explains everything.
- Natural resources guarantee wealth.
- Institutions can change norms.
- Asia used one development model.
- Context changes policy results.

Each reveal needs one sentence and one source.

### 6. Cause ranking

Ask readers to rank education, industry, colonial history, institutions, geography and culture. Repeat at the end.

## Counterexample visuals

### Theory-failure stamp

Use a visible prompt:

> Our first explanation fails here. Why?

Then show which part of the theory needs revision.

### Variation trajectories

Show three Asian cases on identical axes:

- fast growth;
- middle path;
- slower path.

Do the same for African cases where data permit.

## Transfer visuals

### Capability ladder

```text
Basic production -> Quality -> Process improvement -> Design -> Technology -> Brand
```

Use examples of African firms or sectors already moving up the ladder.

### Adapt versus copy

Split the page:

- **Adapt the mechanism:** skills, reliable power, firm learning, export standards.
- **Cannot simply copy:** geopolitics, historical state formation, population density, colonial borders.

## Production checklist

For every visual:

- What question does it answer?
- What source supports it?
- What are the units and dates?
- Is the comparison fair?
- Could the visual create a stereotype?
- Does a static alternative exist for interactive content?
- Is the visual readable on paper and on a classroom screen?
